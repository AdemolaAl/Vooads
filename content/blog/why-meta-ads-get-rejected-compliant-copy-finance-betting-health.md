Few things slow a media buyer down like a rejected ad. You lose hours, your launch gets delayed and repeated rejections can put your whole ad account at risk.

Regulated verticals get hit the hardest. Finance, trading, betting and health all face stricter review. But most rejections come from a short list of avoidable mistakes.

This guide explains why ads get rejected and how to write copy that is clear, honest and far more likely to pass. Always check Meta's current Advertising Standards for your exact case, because rules change and vary by market.

## The most common reasons for rejection

### 1. Personal attributes

Meta does not allow ads that assert or imply someone's personal traits. That includes health conditions, financial status, religion and more.

- **Rejected style:** "Are you struggling with debt?"
- **Rejected style:** "Lose your belly fat now."
- **Safer:** "Tools to help you plan your monthly budget."
- **Safer:** "A simple routine for staying active at home."

The fix: describe the product and what it does. Leave the viewer's condition out of it.

### 2. Unrealistic or guaranteed outcomes

Promises of guaranteed money, fast results or no risk are a major red flag.

- "Make $500 a day from your phone"
- "Guaranteed profit with our signals"
- "Lose 10 kg in 2 weeks"

These claims mislead people and are often illegal too.

### 3. Misleading or sensational content

Fake news layouts, shocking images, fake system alerts and clickbait that does not match the landing page all trigger rejections.

### 4. Before-and-after images

In health and weight loss, before-and-after images are heavily restricted. They often imply unrealistic results.

### 5. Restricted categories without approval

Some categories need written permission or authorisation before you can advertise, such as online gambling and certain financial products. Running them without approval leads to rejection or worse.

### 6. Landing page problems

Meta reviews where your ad leads. Broken pages, pages that do not match the ad, pop-ups that block content and cloaking will all get you rejected. Cloaking can get your account banned.

## Writing compliant copy: finance and trading

Finance ads must be balanced. Show the value without hiding the risk.

**Do:**
- Describe the product and its features.
- Use educational angles, such as "learn how charts work".
- Include risk warnings where the law or the broker requires them.
- Follow the broker's or network's approved claims list.

**Do not:**
- Promise returns or income.
- Show account balances or payout screenshots as typical results.
- Use urgency like "last chance to get rich".
- Imply the viewer is in financial trouble.

**Example rewrite:**

- **Before:** "Turn $10 into $1,000 with this trading app."
- **After:** "Practice trading on a free demo account before you invest real money. Trading involves risk."

## Writing compliant copy: betting and gambling

Gambling ads face strict rules and often need prior approval. Where they are allowed, they must be responsible.

**Do:**
- Confirm the offer is licensed in each market you target.
- Target only adults and respect age limits.
- Focus on entertainment, features and odds information.
- Include responsible gambling messages where required.

**Do not:**
- Present betting as a way to make money or pay bills.
- Target or appeal to minors with cartoon styles or youth slang.
- Promise wins or "sure bets".
- Run in markets where gambling is illegal.

**Example rewrite:**

- **Before:** "Quit your job. Win big every weekend with these sure bets."
- **After:** "Live odds on every match this weekend. 18+. Play responsibly."

## Writing compliant copy: health and wellness

Health ads must avoid personal attributes, miracle claims and misleading visuals.

**Do:**
- Describe ingredients, features and how people use the product.
- Use general wellness language.
- Keep claims you can prove, and only those the law allows.

**Do not:**
- Claim to cure, treat or prevent disease.
- Use before-and-after images.
- Show fake doctors or fake clinical results.
- Body-shame or point at the viewer's body.

**Example rewrite:**

- **Before:** "Doctors hate this! Cure your diabetes naturally."
- **After:** "A low-sugar snack made with simple ingredients."

## A pre-launch checklist

Before you submit any ad in a regulated vertical, ask:

1. Does the copy say or imply something about the viewer's personal traits?
2. Does it promise income, profit, wins or health results?
3. Are any testimonials real and verifiable?
4. Does the visual use before-and-after, shock or fake interface elements?
5. Does the landing page match the ad and work properly?
6. Do I have any approval or licence this category needs?
7. Are required warnings and age limits in place?

If you answer "yes" to any of the first four, rewrite before you launch.

## What to do after a rejection

- **Read the reason.** Meta tells you which policy it flagged.
- **Fix the issue.** Edit the copy, visual or landing page.
- **Request a review** only if you believe the decision is wrong. Do not resubmit the same ad again and again.
- **Do not try to trick the system.** Misspelling words or swapping in symbols to get past review is a policy violation and puts your account at risk.
