A UGC ad has one job in its first line: make the right person stop. If the hook fails, the rest of the script never gets a chance.

The good news is that hooks follow patterns. Once you know the formulas, you can write ten in the time it used to take to write one. Then you test them and let your audience pick the winner.

Below are 12 hook formulas with examples from different verticals. After that, a simple script structure to put them in.

## The 12 hook formulas

### 1. The problem call-out

Name a problem your buyer feels, in their words.

> "If your phone dies before lunch every day, this is for you."

Works best when the problem is specific and daily.

### 2. The "I was wrong" confession

Admit a mistake the viewer is probably making too.

> "I wasted months doing my skincare in the wrong order."

People lean in to hear what the mistake was.

### 3. The unexpected result

Lead with an outcome that sounds surprising, then show how.

> "My delivery costs dropped once I changed one setting."

Keep it honest. Never promise results you cannot prove, especially in finance or health.

### 4. The direct audience call

Point at exactly who the ad is for.

> "New drivers, listen to this before you buy insurance."

This filters out the wrong viewers and pulls in the right ones.

### 5. The question

Ask something your buyer will answer "yes" to in their head.

> "Do you also forget to drink water until 4pm?"

Avoid questions that can be answered "no" and dismissed.

### 6. The "stop doing this"

Tell the viewer to stop a habit.

> "Stop buying cheap chargers. Here's why."

Negative framing grabs attention. Follow it with a clear, fair reason.

### 7. The comparison

Put two options side by side.

> "I tried the expensive one and the cheap one. Here's the difference."

Great for products where buyers already compare options.

### 8. The demo-first

Open on the product doing its thing, with a short line.

> "Watch this stain disappear in ten seconds."

Show, then talk. Strong for physical products with a visual result.

### 9. The list tease

Promise a short list.

> "Three things I wish I knew before I started trading."

Lists set clear expectations, so people stay to the end. For trading and betting, keep the content educational and include risk warnings where required.

### 10. The objection flip

Start with the reason people do not buy, then answer it.

> "I thought this app was a scam too. Then I read the reviews."

Only use reviews and claims that are real and verifiable.

### 11. The "day in my life" opener

Set a relatable scene.

> "Six a.m., two kids, one coffee, and this is how I get dinner sorted."

This feels native to the feed and slows the scroll.

### 12. The secret or insider

Promise inside knowledge.

> "What restaurant owners don't tell you about delivery apps."

Make sure the reveal is genuinely useful, or viewers feel tricked and the comments turn on you.

## A simple UGC script structure

A good hook needs a body that keeps its promise. Here is a structure that works for most short UGC ads of 20 to 40 seconds.

1. **Hook (0 to 3 seconds).** One of the formulas above.
2. **Problem (3 to 8 seconds).** Make the pain real. Use specific details.
3. **Discovery (8 to 15 seconds).** Introduce the product as the thing that helped.
4. **Proof (15 to 25 seconds).** Show it working. Use a demo, a feature, a real review or a clear benefit.
5. **Offer and CTA (last 5 seconds).** Tell the viewer exactly what to do: "Tap the link to get yours" or "Download it free today".

### Full example: food delivery app

> **Hook:** "Stop paying full price for delivery."
> **Problem:** "I used to spend so much on delivery fees every month. It added up fast."
> **Discovery:** "Then a friend showed me this app. It shows you the fees before you order."
> **Proof:** "Same restaurants, and I can pick the cheapest option every time. Look, this order had no delivery fee."
> **CTA:** "It's free to download. Link below."

### Full example: language learning app

> **Hook:** "If you've quit learning a language three times, this is for you."
> **Problem:** "I always gave up after a week. Too much grammar, too boring."
> **Discovery:** "This app gives you five-minute lessons with real conversations."
> **Proof:** "I've done it every day this month. I actually remember the words now."
> **CTA:** "Try your first lesson free. Tap below."

## Rules for writing hooks that last

- **Write how people talk.** Read it out loud. If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.
- **Be specific.** "Before lunch" beats "quickly". "Three things" beats "some tips".
- **Match the hook to the market.** A joke that lands in one language can fall flat in another. Rewrite the idea so it makes sense locally.
- **Stay compliant.** No guaranteed income, no miracle cures, no fake testimonials. Platforms reject these and they hurt your ad account.
- **Test in batches.** Write 5 to 10 hooks for the same body. Change only the hook so you know what made the difference.

## A note on AI presenters

UGC-style ads work because they feel personal. With AI presenters, you can test many hooks with different faces and voices without booking a creator each time. Treat them as scripted spokespeople. Do not present them as real customers, and add an AI label where the platform or law requires it.
