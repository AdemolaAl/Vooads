A slideshow ad is a set of images shown one after another, with short text on each and usually music or a voiceover. On TikTok it might be a photo carousel people swipe through. On Meta it might be a short video built from stills, or a carousel of cards.

Slideshows look simple, and that is the point. They are quick to make, cheap to iterate and easy to read with the sound off. Ecommerce advertisers have been using more of them on TikTok in 2026, and they work well as a testing tool across platforms.

This post covers when slideshows make sense, a slide-by-slide structure that holds attention, how to write the text, how to choose images, and how to use slideshows to find winning angles before you put budget into UGC video.

## Why slideshows still work

**They read well on mute.** Every slide carries text. Nobody needs sound to follow the story.

**They feel native.** Photo carousels and listicle slideshows look like the posts people already swipe through. A good one does not shout "ad" on frame one.

**They are cheap to test.** A slideshow uses images, short text and an optional voiceover. You can make five variations of an idea for the cost of one talking video.

**They are easy to change.** Swap the first slide and you have a new hook. Swap the order and you have a new structure. That makes them ideal for testing angles.

**They carry lower AI-label risk when built from real product photos.** Platforms focus their AI labels on realistic synthetic people and scenes. A slideshow of genuine product photos with text overlays usually falls outside that. If you use AI-generated images of people, follow the platform's disclosure settings.

## When to use a slideshow

- **Angle discovery.** You have five possible reasons to buy and want to know which one people care about.
- **Listicles and education.** "3 things I check before buying sunscreen", "5 mistakes new traders make".
- **Product ranges.** Shades, flavours, sizes, colours.
- **Comparison.** Old way versus new way, step by step.
- **Retargeting.** A quick reminder of benefits, offer and reviews you actually have.
- **Markets where you have no presenter yet.** A slideshow can test a new geo before you commit to a localised UGC video.

When a slideshow is the wrong choice:

- When the product needs to be seen moving or working. A demo video does that better.
- When trust depends on a human face. Some offers need a person explaining.
- When the story only works with a voice and emotion. Slides carry facts better than feelings.

## The slide-by-slide structure

Most strong slideshows follow the same arc as a UGC script, just in slides. For 5 to 7 slides:

| Slide | Job | Example (sunscreen) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | "Sunscreen that does not leave a white cast" |
| 2 | Problem | "Most SPF looks grey on deeper skin" |
| 3 | What it is | "Lightweight gel, SPF on the label" |
| 4 | Proof you can see | Close-up of texture on a hand |
| 5 | Detail 2 | "Sinks in, no shine" |
| 6 | Objection | "Wears under makeup" |
| 7 | CTA | "Find your size at the link" |

For a listicle, slide 1 is the promise ("3 things I check before buying any skincare") and slides 2 to 4 are the points, with the product shown as the one that passes all three.

### Timing

On a video slideshow, give each slide about 1.5 to 3 seconds. The hook slide can be slightly shorter to get the first change on screen quickly. Text-heavy slides need longer. A rough rule: people read about 3 to 4 words per second on a phone, so a 6-word slide needs about 2 seconds.

A 7-slide slideshow at about 2 seconds each is a 14 to 15-second ad. That is a good default.

On a swipeable carousel, the viewer sets the pace, so the hook slide matters even more. If slide 1 does not make them swipe, nothing else gets seen.

## Writing slide text

### Keep it short

3 to 7 words for the headline on each slide. One idea per slide. If you need two sentences, you need two slides.

### Make slide 1 do the hook's job

The same rules as a video hook apply. Name the viewer or their problem, open a loop, and make the promise specific:

- "Stop buying protein that tastes like chalk"
- "3 things every new puppy owner should buy"
- "My tiny kitchen just got bigger"
- "Freelancers: stop chasing invoices"

Avoid starting with the brand name. People care about their problem first.

### Use the viewer's words

Write the way your buyer would describe the problem to a friend. "My phone dies by lunch" beats "battery optimisation".

### Number your lists

"1 of 3" or a big numeral helps people know where they are and how much is left. It increases the chance they keep swiping.

### Keep claims honest

Slides are easy to screenshot. Every claim on them should be true and compliant. No invented review counts, no fake ratings, no before/after for health, weight or skin, no income figures for finance offers. If you have a real rating or a real award, use it exactly as it is.

## Choosing images

**Use real product photos when you have them.** They are trusted, they show the actual product and they keep labelling simple.

**Fill the frame.** Vertical 9:16 for Stories, Reels and TikTok. 4:5 for Feed. Leave space for the text and keep the bottom fifth clear of important details, since platform buttons sit there.

**Change something on every slide.** A new angle, a close-up, a different setting. If slides 2 and 3 look the same, people assume nothing new is coming.

**Show hands and use.** Product in hand, product being used, product in its real setting. Flat packshots on white work for slide 3, less well for slide 1.

**Keep the style consistent.** Same font, same text position, same colour treatment across slides. Consistency makes it feel designed; variety in the images keeps it moving.

**When you generate images,** keep them realistic and accurate to the product. Do not generate fake results, fake people posing as customers or anything that misrepresents what the buyer gets.

## Sound and voiceover

A slideshow can run with only music. Many perform better with a short voiceover that reads the slide text or adds to it.

- **Music only:** fine for ranges, aesthetics and quick listicles. Pick a track that fits the platform and that you are licensed to use.
- **Voiceover:** better for education, comparisons and anything that needs a human tone. Keep it short and matched to the slides.
- **Captions:** if there is a voiceover, put the key words on screen anyway. Many people watch muted.

## Using slideshows to find angles

This is where slideshows earn their keep. Before you spend on UGC video, test angles with slides.

1. **List 4 to 6 angles.** Different reasons to buy: saves time, looks better, costs less than the alternative, makes a good gift, works in a specific situation.
2. **Make one slideshow per angle.** Same images where possible. Change slide 1 and the text on slides 2 to 4 to match the angle.
3. **Launch them together.** Same audience, same budget per ad.
4. **Read the results.** Look at click-through rate and cost per result first. Look at swipe-through or hold rate as a secondary signal.
5. **Turn the winning angle into UGC video.** Now you know what to say. Write 10 hooks for that angle and make your first videos.
6. **Keep the slideshow running.** A winning slideshow often keeps working next to the video versions. Different people respond to different formats.

This approach separates two questions that often get mixed up: "Is this the right message?" and "Is this the right format?" Slideshows answer the first question cheaply. Video then answers the second.

## Slideshow variations to test

Once you have a winner, vary it in ways that change more than one word:

- **New hook slide.** Same body, different first slide. The cheapest high-impact test.
- **Different order.** Put the proof slide second instead of fourth.
- **Listicle versus story.** "3 reasons" against "How I found this".
- **With voiceover versus music only.**
- **Real photos versus generated lifestyle images.**
- **Shorter cut.** Drop to 4 slides. See if the message survives.

Ad delivery systems tend to group near-identical ads, so changes need to be meaningful. A new hook slide with a new image counts. Changing one word on slide 5 usually does not.

## Localising slideshows

Slideshows are the easiest format to take into new markets, because there is no lip-sync to redo.

- Rewrite the text natively in the new language. Do not translate idioms word for word.
- Check text length. Some languages run longer than English. Shorten instead of shrinking the font.
- Change the currency, sizes and measures to local formats.
- Swap images where the setting or people would feel foreign in the new market.
- Check that the product and claims are allowed in that market.

## Quick checklist

- One idea per slide, 3 to 7 words.
- Slide 1 is the hook. Save the logo for later.
- 5 to 7 slides at about 2 seconds each for video slideshows.
- Every slide changes something visually.
- Real product photos where possible; accurate generated images where not.
- Honest claims only. No fake ratings or invented numbers.
- Bottom fifth kept clear for platform buttons.
- Test angles with slideshows, then scale the winner into video.

Slideshows are easy to underrate because they are easy to make. Used as a testing tool, they tell you what to say before you spend on how to say it, and many of them keep converting long after the test is over.
