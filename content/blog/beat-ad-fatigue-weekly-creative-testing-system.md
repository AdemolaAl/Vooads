Every winning ad has an expiry date. You find a creative that prints. CPA is low, click-through is strong, and you scale. Then, a week or three later, results slip. Cost per result climbs. Frequency creeps up. You raise the budget to compensate and it gets worse.

That is ad fatigue. Your audience has seen the ad enough times that it stops working. The platform has to reach colder, more expensive people to spend your budget.

You cannot stop fatigue. You can get ahead of it. The buyers who stay profitable treat creative as a weekly production line. They do not wait for the winner to die before looking for the next one.

Here is a simple system you can run every week.

## Know the signs early

Do not wait for CPA to double. Watch for early signals in your winning ad sets:

- **Frequency rising** in prospecting campaigns, especially when reach growth slows.
- **Click-through rate dropping** week over week on the same creative.
- **Hook rate falling.** This is the share of impressions that watch the first 3 seconds. A falling hook rate means people recognise the ad and scroll.
- **Comments turning stale** or repeating "seen this before".

Pick two or three of these and check them on the same day each week. Trends matter more than single days.

## The weekly testing rhythm

The goal is simple. Every week, a new batch of creatives enters testing. The best ones graduate to scaling. The losers get cut fast.

### Monday: pick the angle

Start from what already works. Look at your current winner and ask why it works. Is it the pain point? The offer? The presenter? The format?

Then choose one variable to test this week. Good options:

- A new pain point for the same product
- A new audience segment, such as beginners vs experienced users
- A new objection to answer, such as price, trust or time
- A new format, such as a talking-head UGC video vs a static before-and-after

Testing one variable at a time tells you what moved the result.

### Tuesday: write hooks

The hook is the first line or first visual. It decides whether anyone sees the rest of your ad. Write 8 to 10 hooks for this week's angle. Mix styles:

- A question: "Still paying for things you never use?"
- A bold statement: "I stopped doing this and my mornings changed."
- A call-out: "If you trade on your phone, watch this."
- A result: "Here is what one week with this looked like."

Keep the body of the ad the same across hooks at first. That way, you learn which opening wins.

### Wednesday: produce

Turn your hooks into creatives. For each hook, aim for:

- One vertical UGC video (9:16) for Reels and Stories
- One square or 4:5 version for Feed
- One static image with the hook as the headline

If you run in several languages, produce your top hooks in your main language first. Translate only after a hook proves itself.

### Thursday: launch tests

Put new creatives into a dedicated testing campaign or ad set. Keep your budget per creative steady, so each one gets a fair chance. A common approach is to give each new creative enough budget to reach a meaningful number of impressions before you judge it. Set that threshold from your own account history.

Do not drop new creatives into your scaling ad set straight away. Untested ads can steal delivery from your proven winner.

### Friday: review and decide

Look at results from the week's tests and from earlier tests that have now gathered enough data. Sort each creative into three groups:

- **Kill:** clearly below your target on hook rate and cost per result.
- **Watch:** promising but not enough data yet.
- **Graduate:** beating your current winner or close to it. Move these into scaling.

Write down what you learned. "Price objection hooks beat pain hooks for this offer" is worth more than any single ad.

## Keep a creative log

A simple spreadsheet is enough. For each creative, record:

- Date launched
- Angle and hook
- Format and presenter
- Language and market
- Hook rate, CTR, cost per result
- Decision and the reason

After a month, patterns appear. You will see which angles last longest, which presenters fit which markets, and how long your winners usually survive. That lets you plan production before fatigue arrives.

## Refresh winners before you replace them

When a winner starts to fade, you do not always need a brand-new concept. Try cheap refreshes first:

- A new hook on the same body
- A different presenter delivering the same script
- A new background or setting
- New captions or a new on-screen headline
- A shorter cut of the same video

Many buyers find a refreshed version of a proven ad can recover performance for a while. It also costs much less than starting from zero.

## How much volume do you need?

There is no fixed number. It depends on your budget, how many ad sets you run and how fast your audiences fatigue. A useful rule of thumb: the more you spend per day on one audience, the faster you need new creatives.

Start with a number you can produce every week without fail. Consistency beats a big batch once a month.
