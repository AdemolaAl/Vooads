You found a winner. The ad is profitable in your main market and you want to take it further. Expanding to new geos with a proven creative is one of the fastest ways to grow, because the angle is already tested.

But a straight translation rarely works on its own. Buyers in a new market notice when an ad was not made for them. Payment methods, prices, slang, faces and even the problem you lead with can change.

Use this checklist before you launch your winner in a new country.

## Step 1: Decide which markets to test first

Do not launch everywhere at once. Pick 2 or 3 markets and learn from them.

Good questions to ask:

- **Does the offer work there?** Is the product available, legal and deliverable?
- **Does the affiliate offer or landing page accept that geo?**
- **What language do people actually use online?** Some markets mix languages. Some prefer English in ads even when another language is official.
- **Is the market similar to your winning one?** Similar buying habits often mean your angle transfers well.

Rank markets by how close they are to your proven one. Start with the closest.

## Step 2: Check the rules before the words

Every market has its own laws and ad-platform rules. This matters most in regulated verticals.

- **Finance and trading:** Some places require risk warnings, licence numbers or forbid certain products entirely.
- **Betting and gambling:** Often allowed only with a licence and with age targeting. Some markets ban it.
- **Health and supplements:** Claims that are fine in one market may be banned in another.
- **Dating, alcohol and others:** Often need platform approval and age limits.

Check the ad platform's policy pages for each market. Check the offer's terms too. Many affiliate networks list restricted geos and banned claims.

## Step 3: Localise the idea behind the words

A literal translation can turn a great hook into a confusing one. Localise these parts of your ad:

- **The hook.** Rewrite it so it sounds natural to a native speaker. Idioms and jokes rarely carry over.
- **The problem.** The pain point may differ. In one market people worry about price. In another they worry about trust or delivery time.
- **Numbers and units.** Use local currency, local date formats and local measurements.
- **Tone.** Some markets respond to direct, energetic delivery. Others prefer calm and polite.
- **Captions.** Most people watch with the sound off. Captions must be accurate, readable and in the right language.

Always get a native speaker to check the final script. AI translation is a strong first draft. A quick human review catches mistakes that cost trust.

## Step 4: Match the presenter to the market

In UGC-style ads, the presenter is part of the message. Viewers relate faster to someone who looks and sounds like their world.

- Choose a presenter whose look fits the audience you target.
- Pick a voice with a natural accent for that language.
- Match the setting. A kitchen, a car, a street or an office should feel familiar.
- Test more than one presenter per market. The one that won at home may not win abroad.

## Step 5: Localise the offer and the path to buy

The ad is only half the job. If the next step feels foreign, people drop off.

- **Price:** Show prices in local currency where you can.
- **Payment methods:** Mention the methods people trust locally, such as mobile money, local cards, Pix, UPI or bank transfer, where your offer supports them.
- **Landing page:** Translate it fully, including buttons, forms and legal text.
- **Support:** If the offer uses a chat or Telegram channel, make sure someone can answer in the local language.
- **Delivery and timing:** Be clear about shipping times or how fast an account is activated.

## Step 6: Build every placement

Each market will see your ad across Feed, Stories, Reels and other placements. Prepare:

- 9:16 for Stories and Reels
- 4:5 or 1:1 for Feed
- 1.91:1 where landscape placements apply
- Static versions of your top hooks for every size

Keep text inside safe zones so it is not hidden by buttons or profile names.

## Step 7: Launch with a clean structure

- Create a separate campaign or ad set per market, so results do not mix.
- Use the same test budget per market at first, so you can compare fairly.
- Give each market enough time and spend to gather real data before you judge it.
- Track results by market and by language as well as in total.

## Step 8: Read results and iterate

After the first test round:

- **If the ad wins:** scale it, and start a new hook test for that market.
- **If the click-through is good but conversions are weak:** the problem is likely the landing page, the offer or the payment flow.
- **If the click-through is weak:** the hook or presenter is not landing. Test new local hooks.
- **If ads get rejected:** review the policy for that market and rewrite the copy.

Keep notes per market. Over time you will build a playbook of what each audience responds to.

## The full checklist

- [ ] Offer available and legal in the market
- [ ] Ad-platform and offer rules checked for the vertical
- [ ] Hook rewritten by or checked with a native speaker
- [ ] Pain point adjusted for local buyers
- [ ] Currency, units and dates localised
- [ ] Presenter, voice and setting fit the market
- [ ] Captions accurate and readable
- [ ] Landing page and buttons translated
- [ ] Local payment methods shown
- [ ] All placements built, text in safe zones
- [ ] Separate campaign or ad set per market
- [ ] Results tracked by market and language
