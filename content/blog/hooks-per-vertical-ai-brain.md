There is no such thing as a universal hook. "Watch what happens when I drop it" is a great opening for a phone case and a strange one for a budgeting app. "Things I wish I knew before my first trade" works for trading education and would feel odd for a pair of sneakers.

Hooks change by vertical for two reasons. Buyers in each niche care about different things, and ad platforms treat each niche differently. A health hook can be rejected for a phrase that is perfectly fine in a home-goods ad.

This post explains what changes from vertical to vertical, gives examples for ten niches, and shows how the Vooads AI brain uses vertical knowledge when it writes hooks, scripts and angles for you.

## What changes between verticals

### The pain

Every vertical has its own daily annoyances. Skincare buyers are tired of routines with too many steps and products that feel heavy. Gadget buyers are tired of dead batteries and tangled cables. SaaS buyers are tired of copy-pasting between tabs. A hook that names the right pain in the buyer's own words is most of the work.

### The proof

What makes a claim believable differs too. In gadgets, a demo is the proof. In skincare, texture and finish on camera. In SaaS, a screen recording. In trading education, a clear curriculum and a risk line do more for trust than any promise.

### The rules

This is where most hooks go wrong. Some verticals carry heavy platform restrictions:

- **Health, supplements and weight management:** no medical claims, no before/after, no time-bound results, no lines that assume the viewer has a condition or a body issue.
- **Finance, trading and crypto:** no income claims, no guaranteed returns, often a required risk line, and advertiser verification in many countries.
- **Gambling and sweepstakes:** only where legal and licensed, 18+ messaging, nothing that suggests gambling fixes money problems or appeals to minors.
- **Personal attributes everywhere:** on Meta especially, a hook that asserts something personal about the viewer ("Are you in debt?", "Struggling with your weight?") is a common rejection.

A good hook in a restricted vertical gets attention while staying inside those lines. That usually means talking about the product, the routine, the process or the presenter's own view, instead of the viewer's problem as a personal trait.

## Hooks for 10 verticals

Each set below shows a few hook patterns that tend to fit the niche, and one risky line rewritten into a safer one. These are starting points to test. None of them is a guaranteed winner.

### Skincare

- "I was putting my serum on in the wrong order."
- "Sunscreen that does not leave a white cast. Watch."
- "3 things I check on every skincare label."

Risky: "This will clear your acne." Safer: "My routine went from eight steps to three. Here is what stayed."

### Supplements

- "Stop buying protein that tastes like chalk."
- "Watch this mix with no shaker."
- "Everything in one scoop. Here is the label."

Risky: "Burns belly fat while you sleep." Safer: "I take it with my morning coffee. Here is why I switched."

### Fitness

- "My whole home gym fits under the bed."
- "20 minutes, no commute, done."
- "Stop guessing what to train today."

Risky: "Lose 10 kilos in 30 days." Safer: "This plan tells me exactly what to do each day."

### Ecommerce gadgets

- "If your phone dies before lunch, watch this."
- "Okay, I did not expect it to fold this small."
- "This replaced three cables on my desk."

Risky: "Was $199, now $29 for today only" (with a made-up original price). Safer: show the real price, the real offer, and the product doing its job.

### Home

- "Here is how hotel cleaners get towels this soft."
- "No drilling. No landlord drama."
- "Watch this lift a red wine stain."

Risky: "Kills 99.9% of germs" (without certified proof). Safer: "Watch how much dust this picks up from one shelf."

### Pets

- "Why does my dog pull on every walk?"
- "My cat ignored every toy until this one."
- "Watch how much hair this picks up."

Risky: "Cures your dog's anxiety." Safer: "Our walks got a lot calmer once we switched to this lead."

### SaaS

- "I used to spend Friday afternoon on reports."
- "This replaced three tabs I kept open all day."
- "Watch me set this up in under two minutes."

Risky: "Double your revenue in a month." Safer: "My follow-ups send themselves now. Here is the setup."

### Fintech and budgeting

- "This app shows where every bit of my money goes."
- "I found three subscriptions I forgot about."
- "Sending money home used to take days."

Risky: "Drowning in debt? This fixes it." Safer: "Payday, and I already have a plan for every bit of it."

### Trading and investing education

- "Learn how charts work before you risk real money."
- "Things I wish I knew before my first trade."
- "3 rules I follow before opening any trade."

Risky: "I make $500 a day with this strategy." Safer: "The first thing I learned was how to lose less. Trading involves risk."

### iGaming (licensed markets, 18+)

- "Matchday odds and live scores in one app."
- "Here is how cash-out works, step by step."
- "Set your limits before you start. Here is where."

Risky: "Win big tonight and quit your job." Safer: "The app I open on derby day. 18+. Play responsibly."

## Tips for better vertical hooks

**Give the writer specifics.** "Sunscreen" gets generic hooks. "Lightweight gel sunscreen with no white cast, for people in hot climates" gets hooks about texture, heat and finish.

**Name the audience.** "For freelancers who invoice clients abroad" changes the pain, the language and the examples.

**Supply real proof.** If you have a real rating, a real certification or a real guarantee, include it in your product description. The writer will only use proof you provide.

**Pick the language first.** Hooks are written natively in the language you choose. A Spanish hook pack is written in Spanish, with local phrasing.

**Read them out loud.** The best hook usually sounds like something a person would actually say to a friend.

**Throw most of them away.** Twenty hooks are meant to give you choice. Keep the five strongest and test those.

## Quick checklist

- Name the pain in your buyer's words.
- Match the proof to the vertical: demo, texture, screen recording, curriculum.
- In restricted verticals, talk about the product and the process instead of the viewer's personal traits.
- No income, health or guaranteed-result claims.
- Use angles to decide what to say, and hooks to decide how to open.
- Let Ban Shield check everything, including your edits.
- Test, keep what your data supports, and refresh before it fatigues.

A hook is a small piece of text with a big job. Writing it for the vertical, in the buyer's words and inside the platform's rules, is what gives it a fair chance to win.
